# First prototype — PLA, sand, casting and lathe finishing — English main project

> **Status:** canonical prototype route · **Owns:** first prototype cost · **Up:** [M Reloaded](README.md)

The first engine is not machined from solid. The geometry is **PLA printed** (1.5 m³), **sand molded** (10 m³ of fine sand), cast in aluminum in a **~3 m³ crucible** (solar furnace with mirrors or contract foundry), then **lathe finished** (~1.40 m diameter). Pessimistic cost: **€52,000–196,000** outsourced, or **€122,000–426,000** including the solar furnace and crucible.

## 1. Why not the lathe first?

Machining a 1.5 m³ engine from solid needs a 2 m lathe, a lot of material and many machining hours. The first prototype must validate geometry and casting, not series production. The lathe comes **after** casting, for fine finishing.

## 2. Manufacturing route

1. **PLA printing** — lost pattern or reusable pattern, 1.5 m³ envelope, 10–20 % effective material (≈ 190–370 kg PLA). Rough XXL price: €100–200/kg, i.e. €20,000–75,000.
2. **Sand mold** — 10 m³ of fine sand (≈ 16 t), binder, mixing, setup: €3,000–8,000.
3. **Casting** — molten aluminum in a ~3 m³ crucible. Two options:
   - contract foundry: €10,000–30,000 per cast;
   - solar furnace + crucible + mirrors (polished stainless-steel plates): €70,000–230,000 capex, reusable.
4. **Lathe finishing** — 2 m lathe, ~1.40 m part, 30–80 h: €3,000–20,000.
5. **Assembly** — piston, connecting rod (3 high-end bearings), cylinder, fittings: €1,200–3,000.
6. **Engineering** — 300–600 h: €15,000–60,000.

The furnace and crucible are reusable: shared over 10 prototypes, their share falls to €7,000–23,000 per prototype. The first prototype itself therefore remains ≈ **€52,000–196,000**; the furnace is a workshop investment.

## 4. What it replaces

The engine replaces an **air-water heat pump, 15–30 kW** (€15,000–35,000, heat only) or a **compact tractor, 20–40 hp** (€25,000–60,000, mechanical work). At **€19,999**, it sits in the heat-pump range and below the price of a compact tractor, while delivering ~1.5 kW mechanical + ~29 kW thermal. See [business costs](../../../business/costs.md#4-what-the-engine-replaces).

Caution: the 5 % mechanical efficiency is not a heat-pump COP. A heat pump moves 2–4 kWh of heat per kWh of electricity; the engine converts 5 % of the straw heat into shaft work and releases the rest as heat. The 20–40 hp tractor delivers much more power: the comparison is price positioning, not power or thermodynamic equivalence.
