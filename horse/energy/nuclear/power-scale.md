# Nuclear power scale

> **Status:** preliminary summary · **Owns:** nuclear power-scale argument · **Up:** [Nuclear variant](README.md)

The point is not to build a large reactor, but to replace a few kilowatts of straw heat with a compact, long-lived source.

- **1 ch ≈ 736 W mechanical.** At the 5 % baseline that is ~14.7 kW thermal; at 15 % it is ~4.9 kW.
- **4 ch ≈ 3 kW mechanical.** At 5 % that is ~59 kW thermal; at 15 % it is ~20 kW.
- The source is not explosive; it is a continuous heat source whose output decays over time.
- Heat rejection can use the open Manson cycle and a radiator/air flow.
- Shielding dominates the mass and volume. Lead handles gamma; neutrons need a hydrogenous material.

The older source note uses 20 cm of lead; the canonical working figure is **30 cm** for gamma, with a separate neutron shield. See [nuclear hub](README.md) and [raw marketing note](notes/marketing.md).
