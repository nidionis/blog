# Nuclear power scale

> **Status:** preliminary summary · **Owns:** nuclear power-scale argument · **Up:** [Nuclear variant](README.md) · **Lang:** EN · [FR](../../2cheval/energy/nuclear/power-scale.md)

The point is not to build a large reactor, but to replace a few kilowatts of straw heat with a compact, long-lived source. The studied range is **1–12 hp**, with **4 hp** as the reference and **1 hp** as the simplest shielded package.

- **1 ch ≈ 736 W mechanical.** At 5 % that is ~14.7 kW thermal; at 15 % ~4.9 kW.
- **4 ch ≈ 3 kW mechanical.** At 5 % that is ~59 kW thermal; at 15 % ~20 kW.
- **12 ch ≈ 8.8 kW mechanical.** At 5 % that is ~177 kW thermal; at 15 % ~59 kW. This is the upper exploratory bound, not a recommendation.
- The source is not explosive; it is a continuous heat source whose output decays over time.
- Heat rejection can use the open Manson cycle and a radiator/air flow.
- Shielding dominates the mass and volume. Lead handles gamma; neutrons need a hydrogenous material.

The older source note uses 20 cm of lead; the canonical working figure is **30 cm** for gamma, with a separate neutron shield. See [nuclear hub](README.md) and [raw marketing note](notes/marketing.md).
