# Nuclear danger note (English-only)

> **Status:** source note · **Not normative** · **Canonical summary:** [safety](../safety.md)

This analysis evaluates the danger of a small 200-gram pellet of highly radioactive material, two weeks old, unshielded, at 500–600 °C, assuming the pellet is in open air.

1. At direct contact (0 cm)
   - Thermal danger: the pellet is an absolute burn source. At 500 °C or 600 °C, simply touching or approaching it instantly carbonises the skin.
   - Radiological danger: cataclysmic dose rate. In direct contact, ~2 000–5 000 Sv/h. A fraction of a second can be lethal.

2. At 5 centimetres
   - Thermal danger: intense radiant heat. Approaching the hand to 5 cm gives an unbearable burning sensation.
   - Radiological danger: the dose rate drops quickly, but remains immense, tens to hundreds of Sv/h. Staying a few seconds at 5 cm would expose to a lethal dose (over 5 Sv). Dead on contact zone.

3. At 2 metres
   - Thermal danger: heat dissipates in room air; imperceptible.
   - Radiological danger: inverse-square law, radiation decreases significantly but remains extremely dangerous. Dose rate drops to a few Sv/h. Only a few minutes would accumulate a lethal dose of 5 Sv.

4. At 10 metres
   - Thermal danger: totally imperceptible.
   - Radiological danger: radiation massively attenuated (about 25× lower than at 2 m). mSv/h or tens of mSv/h. Immediate death unlikely; long-term risks persist.
   - Isolable with a sheet-metal thickness of about 15 cm of lead.
