# Nuclear safety distances

> **Status:** preliminary summary · **Owns:** safety summary · **Up:** [Nuclear variant](README.md) · **Lang:** EN · [FR](../../2cheval/energy/nuclear/safety.md)

The source note analyses a **200 g** highly radioactive pellet, two weeks old, unshielded, at 500–600 °C. The table below is a summary, not a safety procedure.

| Distance | Thermal risk | Radiological risk | Verdict |
|---:|---|---|---|
| Contact (0 cm) | Instant carbonisation | Hundreds of Sv/h | A fraction of a second can be lethal |
| 5 cm | Intense radiant heat | Tens to hundreds of Sv/h | A few seconds can be lethal |
| 2 m | Not perceptible | A few Sv/h | Minutes can accumulate a lethal dose |
| 10 m | Not perceptible | mSv/h to tens of mSv/h | Immediate death unlikely; long-term risk remains |

A few tens of centimetres of lead can isolate the gamma component; neutron shielding is a separate problem. See the [raw safety note](notes/danger.md) and the [shielding discussion](notes/radio-isotope.md).

**Do not use this page as an operational safety procedure.**
